## S1. Annotation codebook

The classification prompts (`prompts/`) operationalise this codebook; the construct boundaries and anchor examples below reached the adapted models through supervised training on codebook-labelled articles.

The codebook (version 3, April 2026) was frozen before annotation began; its label
definitions, construct boundaries, decision rules and valence scale are reproduced below. The
wording of the operational classification prompts (`prompts/`) reached its final version on the
training split only, never on the held-out evaluation set, which was scored once after the
codebook and prompts had stabilised. The inter-annotator and model-versus-reference agreement statistics measure the reliability with which the shared rubric of codebook and prompts was applied, not criterion validity against an external 5C instrument (see main-text limitations).

**Document-level metadata.**
- *Unit of analysis:* the complete article.
- *Construct framework:* the 5C framework (Betsch et al.) as operationalised in ECDC's social
  and behavioural science tools for promoting vaccination acceptance and uptake (Stockholm:
  ECDC; 2025), reframed here at the article level as media-*framing* domains. Labels are a
  property of the article's framing, not of the attitudes of journalists, cited sources or
  readers.
- *Coders and procedure:* two annotators (a senior and a junior public-health specialist)
  independently double-annotated every article. They were trained on the codebook in a
  dedicated session; after the first 100 articles, coding was compared and calibrated through
  structured discussion, without changes to the frozen definitions, and the remaining articles were then completed under the calibrated rules. Annotation used the full article text, so outlet and publication
  date are intrinsic to the material and were not blinded. Disagreements at every level were
  first addressed by consensus discussion between the two annotators; any that remained unresolved were adjudicated by a third public-health specialist, giving one adjudicated label set for all 400 articles.
- *Inter-annotator agreement* (Cohen's κ, before adjudication): relevance 0.70 (n = 400), any-5C presence 0.61 (n = 222 mutually relevant articles), primary disease 0.91 (n = 212).
- *Codebook/prompt version:* relevance classifier `relevance-gate-v2-broad-vaccination-2026-05-14`;
  extractor (Stage-2) system prompt v7.

**Corpus note.** The archive was compiled by the media-monitoring provider under a standing keyword brief: an item entered if it appeared in a monitored Slovenian mainstream-media outlet and matched the provider's keyword set for vaccination, vaccines or vaccine-preventable diseases. Entry was a keyword match, so the archive can contain items matching the brief but not about vaccination, or covering a vaccine-preventable disease without its vaccination component; relevance was adjudicated by the annotators. Before sampling, exact duplicate clippings (the same article from the same outlet) were removed, while the same article appearing in different outlets was retained as a separate item. The provider is not named, in line with institutional policy.

### S1.1 Variable: Relevance (`relevance`)

- **Values:** `relevant`, `not_relevant`.
- **Conceptual definition:** whether vaccines or vaccination are discussed as part of the
  article's actual content.
- **Coding rule (broad, recall-favouring):** code `relevant` when vaccines or vaccination are
  discussed as article content from any point of view (brief, factual, neutral, logistical,
  administrative, procurement-related, policy-related, critical, supportive or sceptical), at
  any length beyond a bare undeveloped word mention. A strong 5C signal is *not* required for
  relevance (a relevant article may have `has_relevant_5c = no`).
- **Inclusion cues:** vaccination/vaccines directly; uptake, refusal, hesitancy, acceptance,
  confidence, safety, effectiveness, benefit, risk, side effects, misinformation; access,
  availability, logistics, eligibility, cost, appointments, schedule, delivery, stock,
  procurement, donation, waste, recommendations, mandates, campaigns, certificates; disease
  risk/outbreak/prevention explicitly connected to vaccination; routine childhood, adult or
  booster vaccination or vaccination programmes.
- **Exclusion / boundaries (`not_relevant`):** disease names alone; general health,
  prevention, infection, treatment, screening, hospitals, politics or pandemic context alone;
  public-health institutions alone; vaccination appearing only as a bare word mention, list
  item or undeveloped background aside. If uncertain between "bare mention" and "discussed as
  content", choose `relevant`.

The human annotators recorded only the relevance decision (`relevant` / `not_relevant`). The machine relevance classifier also emits a `reason_code` (`prompts/relevance_gate_prompt_v2.md`).

### S1.2 Variable: Primary disease anchor (`disease_1`)

- **Values:** `covid19`, `influenza`, `measles`, `hpv`, `unspecified`, `other`.
- **Coding rule:** choose one primary disease anchor. Use `other` for another clear
  vaccine-preventable disease or vaccine group (including paediatric or routine childhood
  vaccination). Use `unspecified` only when vaccination is meaningfully discussed in generic
  terms with no clearer vaccine or disease anchor.

### S1.3 Variable: Any-5C presence (`has_relevant_5c`)

- **Values:** `yes`, `no`.
- **Coding rule:** whether the article carries any article-level 5C framing signal. A relevant
  article may legitimately carry none (`no`).

### S1.4 Variable: Construct-level presence (`c5_presence_d1`)

Zero or more constructs can be present. Each construct
is defined below by a core framing question, its boundary and disambiguation rules, and
illustrative anchor examples for each valence level. Each construct is coded present only
when it is used in a vaccination-relevant way, and the article's *framing* is coded, not
isolated quotes (a quote is coded only in the direction the article itself endorses; a quote
reported at arm's length is non-directional).

**Confidence.** *Core question:* does the article move the reader to trust vaccines, vaccine
safety/effectiveness, or the experts and institutions recommending vaccination more, less, or
neither?
*Boundary:* general political or institutional distrust is coded as Confidence only when
clearly tied to vaccination or to those recommending it. *Disambiguation:* trust in the
vaccine/system = Confidence, whereas perceived disease risk/necessity = Complacency; a bare
safety or efficacy claim = Confidence, and the same claim counts as Calculation only when the
article explicitly stages it as a factor the reader should weigh in a vaccinate-or-not
decision.
*Anchors:* **+1** experts affirm the vaccine is safe and effective; reporting reinforces the
regulator's credibility. **0** a survey of public trust reported descriptively, without
endorsement. **−1** claims the vaccine is unsafe, or that the authorities cannot be trusted on
it.

**Complacency.** *Core question:* does the article frame the disease as making vaccination
more necessary, less necessary, or neither? *Boundary:* disease severity on its own does not
qualify unless it is linked to vaccination relevance. *Disambiguation:* risk-of-disease
framing = Complacency; trust-in-the-vaccine framing = Confidence.
*Anchors:* **+1** rising disease risk used to stress the importance of vaccinating. **0**
disease incidence reported in a vaccination-related article without implying that vaccination is more or less necessary. **−1** disease framed as mild, or
vaccination as unnecessary.

**Constraints.** *Core question:* does the article make getting vaccinated seem easier to
obtain or do, harder, or neither (access, logistics, cost, administrative or practical
barriers)? *Boundary:* purely descriptive logistics with no directional framing (for example opening hours or
locations) are coded Constraints present with non-directional valence (0).
*Anchors:* **+1** expanded access, walk-in clinics, removed barriers. **0** opening hours or
locations stated descriptively. **−1** vaccination portrayed as burdensome; queues, cost or
red tape emphasised.

**Calculation.** *Core question:* does the article guide the reader's weighing of reasons for
and against vaccination towards it, away from it, or neither? *Apply Calculation only* when the article
explicitly stages or guides a vaccination decision process. Typical cues: weighing benefits
and risks; comparing reasons for and against; comparing vaccine options, brands, schedules or
strategies; telling readers to decide after reviewing evidence; summarising evidence to help
decide whether to vaccinate. *Do not use Calculation for:* facts, statistics or side effects
alone; trust or safety claims unless explicitly framed as part of weighing a decision; disease
threat unless the article explicitly compares vaccinating versus not vaccinating; access or
cost unless the article explicitly frames whether vaccination is worth doing despite those
barriers.
*Anchors:* **+1** a risk–benefit comparison that resolves towards vaccinating. **0** an
explicitly balanced "weigh it up / decide for yourself" treatment. **−1** reasoning that
resolves towards delay or refusal.

**Collective Responsibility.** *Core question:* does the article frame vaccination as more, or
less, a way to protect others and society? *Boundary:* a neutral mention of community benefit
without directional framing is non-directional.
*Anchors:* **+1** protecting populations at greater risk; herd-protection framing. **0** a neutral
mention of community protection. **−1** rejection of vaccination as a social responsibility.

### S1.5 Variable: Construct-specific valence (`valence_<construct>_d1`)

For each present construct, the framing direction. The valence scale uses the same codes in the codebook and the prompts:

| Code | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| `1` | Supportive | framing pushes towards vaccination |
| `0` | Non-directional | neutral, descriptive, balanced, mixed or ambivalent vaccination-relevant framing |
| `-1` | Undermining | framing pushes away from vaccination |
| `null` | Not applicable | construct not present in the article |

Code the directional signal embedded in the article text, not the annotator's own opinion. Code `0` is a single category for framing that, on balance, is neither supportive nor undermining, whether neutral, descriptive or two-sided; neutral and mixed framing were not coded separately.

### S1.6 Cross-variable hierarchy rules

- For Stage-2 records, `relevance` = `relevant` and `disease_1` is not null.
- If `has_relevant_5c` = `no`: `c5_presence_d1` = `[]` and every valence field = `null`.
- If `has_relevant_5c` = `yes`: every present construct appears in `c5_presence_d1` with
  valence `-1`, `0` or `1`; every absent construct has valence `null`.
